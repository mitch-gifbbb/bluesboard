Reading the board

Orientation

Left-handed by default: nut on the right, frets running left. The Hand toggle flips the whole neck for a right-handed view, nut on the left. Low E sits along the bottom either way, and the board stops at fret 12 since past the octave everything repeats. Click any dot to hear it.

Two colour changes that look like bugs

  • The red root vanishes when you pick I or IV. In A, the root is the 1 of A7 and the 5 of D7 — so it goes amber. Over E7 it isn't in the chord at all, so it turns red. Red means home, but homeless right now.
  • The blue b3 turns amber over the IV. In A blues the b3 is C, which is the b7 of D7. It stops being a blue note and becomes the most important note in the bar. This is why leaning on that note over the IV always sounds right.

Number from

The oldest argument in improvising, as a toggle: playing the key vs playing the changes. It only changes the numbers and what the Spotlight buttons mean — the colours never move.

  • The key — one fixed map. 1 is always A, b3 is always C, whatever chord is sounding. Use it when the whole progression is one landscape: the classic blues where minor pentatonic covers all twelve bars. It's also how you memorise the neck — labels that stay put become geography. Weakness: it tells you nothing about the change. Over E7 it still calls that note 4. True, and useless.
  • The chord — the map re-spells every time you pick a different chord. The same fret is 4 over the I, 1 over the IV, b7 over the V. Use it when you want to know what a note does right now — guide tones, resolutions, why some notes are arrival and others are transit. Weakness: nothing stays still, so it's a poor way to learn the layout.

The sweet spot: set numbering to the key, then select a chord anyway. Stable geography, and the amber dots still move through the progression — you get the changes as shape, without the labels shifting under you.

Play changes

The board can walk the 12-bar form for you so your hands stay on the guitar. Set your backing track's tempo in BPM, hit Play, and start the track at the same moment — the two run alongside each other. There's no way for the app to hear your track, so it can't lock to it; over many choruses it may drift a hair. Restart snaps it back to bar 1 at the top of any chorus.

  • One board — the neck auto-switches I → IV → V as the bars pass. If you've got guide tones spotlit, watch the two dots move on their own at each change. This is playing the changes without touching a button.
  • Three boards — I, IV and V laid out at once, each numbered from its own root. Reference, not motion. With Play running, the active bar's board lights up and the other two dim, so you can see the change coming.
  • Feel — Standard is the plain 12-bar. Quick change moves to the IV in bar 2, which a lot of tunes do. Click turns on a metronome if you're practising without a track.

Show

These are layers, not modes — stack them. Minor pent under Full minor shows you the safe frame sitting inside the fuller scale. Major pent and Minor pent together shows every place on the neck where b3 and 3 sit a fret apart. That pair is the blues.

Spotlight

Emphasis, not a filter — everything else fades but stays visible.

  • Guide tones — the 3rd and 7th of the chord. Two dots. These spell the change; roots and 5ths don't. If you only chase one thing, chase these.
  • Triad — 1 3 5 of the chord.
  • Roots — just 1. Good for finding your position fast.
  • Bends — marks every note under your fingers that bends up onto a chord tone. The small amber number by the dot is where you land: a 5 means bend this note up to the 5th. Every marked bend lands on the root, 3rd or 5th of the chord, so they're the safe, singing ones. This is phrasing made visible: the amber chord tones are your targets, the marked notes are where you bend from.

The single degree buttons are manual and read against Number from; the named buttons always track the chord itself.

Why bends need a chord: a bend is only "safe" relative to what's sounding — b3→3 is the money bend over the I but a wrong note over the V. Selecting Bends auto-picks the I if nothing's chosen. In Three boards you get all three bend maps at once; hit Play and the safe bends light up on whichever chord is current.

Strings

Clamps the board to three adjacent strings. The board deals in pitch classes — it knows which notes, not which voicing — so on its own it'll show you six different 3s and won't say which one belongs in a shape. Clamping fixes that by removing everything that isn't reachable in one grip.

For triads: layers off, pick a chord, hit Triad, clamp to 1–3. What's left is that triad and its inversions, stacked up the neck in order. Move to 2–4, 3–5, 4–6 and the same three notes re-voice themselves on each string set.

A way to practise with it

Set Blues, pick your key, hit guide tones, and loop I7–IV7–V7. Play nothing but those two notes per chord until you can find them without looking. Then turn the pentatonic back on — you'll be decorating targets instead of running a shape.
